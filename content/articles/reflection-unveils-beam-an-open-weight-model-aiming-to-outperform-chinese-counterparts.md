## Beam’s Launch and Claims
Reflection announced Beam, its first frontier open‑weight model, saying it matches the performance of leading Chinese open models on advanced reasoning benchmarks while running at dramatically lower costs. The startup described Beam as a text‑only mixture‑of‑experts trained with high‑compute reinforcement learning to excel at reasoning, coding, and agentic tasks at a fraction of the token cost and inference time of rivals.

## Competitive Edge and Benchmarks
Reflection’s internal benchmarks show Beam outscores Inkling, the open model from Mira Murati’s Thinking Machines Lab, on four coding tests where both report results. Inkling is multimodal, whereas Beam is text‑only.

## Compute Resources and Partnerships
The company secured more than $7 billion in deals this summer with SpaceX and Nebius to access Nvidia’s GB300 chips through 2029. Nvidia’s CEO, Jensen Huang, who backs Reflection, has championed the “AI factory” idea and aims to strengthen the open AI ecosystem, benefiting Nvidia’s GPU business.

## Target Markets and Release Plans
Reflection aims Beam and future models at enterprises and sovereign nations, positioning them as tools for building localized AI systems. The company will release Beam’s weights and full technical details this month, distributing them through hyperscalers and neoclouds and integrating with open‑source libraries at launch.

## Company Background and Funding
Founded in 2024 by two former Google DeepMind researchers, Reflection has raised roughly $4.7 billion from backers including Nvidia, Sequoia Capital, and Lightspeed Venture Partners.

## What to Watch
Beam claims it matches the performance of leading Chinese open models at dramatically lower costs. The scale of the compute deals and the alignment with Nvidia’s ecosystem suggest a serious push to create a Western alternative to Chinese open models, potentially reshaping competitive dynamics in the AI model marketplace.
