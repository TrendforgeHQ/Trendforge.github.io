## What Changed
Apple announced new restrictions on the macOS "Full Disk Access" setting. The changes will only allow apps that truly need this level of access to obtain it after users perform a very explicit action. The move follows earlier reporting that Meta’s Muse AI could read private messages on a user’s Mac without explicit permission.

## Why It Matters
The Full Disk Access setting lets an app read files, mail, messages, and browsing history. It was originally designed to let backup apps function properly. Apple said the risks associated with this level of access have increased because desktop AI agents can now control system functions and read personal content.

This shift raises questions about the security and trust users place in desktop‑based AI. The setting can bypass normal macOS privacy controls, allowing an app to see content that normally would be protected. Apple emphasized its commitment to ensuring users clearly understand these risks so they can make informed decisions about their data and privacy.

## What Users Should Know
Apple’s update does not remove the ability for apps to get full disk access, but it makes the permission harder to obtain.Apple says users who genuinely wish to grant an app this extraordinary level of access can only do so with very explicit user action. The change is part of a broader industry response to the growing capabilities of AI agents that can access sensitive files.

The announcement comes weeks after a columnist reported that Muse AI somehow knew the contents of his messages on a Mac mini, even though the chatbot was not given explicit permission. Apple has not yet provided detailed guidance on how the new controls will be implemented, and the company has not responded to inquiries from several outlets.

In short, the updated controls are intended to make sure anyone giving an AI agent sweeping access to a Mac is fully aware of what they are authorizing.Apple’s preemptive security move signals a broader industry awakening to the unique risks posed by AI agents. For now, users should review app permissions and consider whether Full Disk Access is truly necessary for the applications they use.
