## A Split View on Bitcoin and Ethereum
Former BlackRock executive Joseph Chalom, who has spent two decades at the firm, spoke on a podcast about the roles of Bitcoin and Ethereum in today’s financial system. He described Bitcoin as an “exit asset” and Ethereum as “the new rails” that could support a future financial ecosystem.

Chalom’s remarks were reported on September 25 2026. He argued that Bitcoin serves as a store‑of‑value vehicle for institutions, while Ethereum’s blockchain could host stablecoins, tokenized funds, bonds, decentralized finance and AI‑powered agents—an innovation he believes could threaten about $4 trillion of financial‑services fees over the next decade.

Bitwise, the manager of the Bitwise Bitcoin ETF (BITB), counters by noting that institutions treat Bitcoin as a primary holding but view Ethereum and Solana more as speculative investments that may be sold if growth stalls.

## ETF Flows Highlight the Divide
Between September 21 and 25, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs received an influx of roughly $2.4 billion, while Ethereum ETFs attracted only $690 million. This stark difference in inflows aligns with the institutional split: Bitcoin continues to draw large commitments, whereas Ethereum’s momentum appears more modest.

If Bitcoin’s inflows stay strong and Ethereum’s decline, Chalom’s narrative may gain traction. Conversely, if Ethereum funds see outflows after disappointing adoption news, Bitwise’s caution would seem more justified.

## What This Means for Investors
The contrasting views signal a broader debate about which crypto will underpin future financial services. If Ethereum truly becomes the backbone for stablecoins, tokenized assets, and DeFi, institutional risk appetites may shift, potentially driving a new wave of capital into Ethereum. On the other hand, Bitcoin’s persistent role as a store of value could continue to attract capital, especially during periods of market uncertainty.
