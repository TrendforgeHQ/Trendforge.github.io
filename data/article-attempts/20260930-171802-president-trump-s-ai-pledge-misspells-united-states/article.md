## The pledge and its wording
On Tuesday, President Donald Trump and top AI leaders announced a signed pledge called a “Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities,” a voluntary promise to implement more controls and safety measures as the rapid development of artificial intelligence continues.
The pledge is part of an executive order signed by Trump that replaces the phrase “artificial intelligence” with “super intelligence,” reaffirming what Trump said in his U.N. address. Under the president’s signature, the text reads “President of the Unites States,” a misspelling that appears right beneath the president’s signature, while other signatories and their companies are spelled correctly.

## Why the error matters
The misspelling of “United States” in a formal government document draws ridicule and raises questions about the document’s drafting process. The error is noted by Gizmodo reporter Matt Novak, who was one of the first to point out the snafu, and the White House has been contacted for comment. The mistake appears to increase ridicule of the accord, as noted by the New Republic.

## Context and implications
The accord followed calls by OpenAI’s Sam Altman, Amodei, and Elon Musk for a deceleration of AI advancements, which President Trump and Huang rejected. Trump has previously stated that the United States is “the most sophisticated country in the world” and that whoever wins AI wins.
The executive order also intersects with other legal actions, such as CAIR’s Florida chapter suing DeSantis over an order that stripped the group’s access to state resources, and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth’s efforts to clear the deck of battle‑tested leaders as he prepares to continue a war effort.

## What to watch
Observers should note how the misspelling may influence the perception of the pledge’s seriousness and whether future AI governance documents will undergo stricter proofreading. The broader debate over AI regulation continues, with leaders on both sides of the spectrum voicing differing views on how quickly progress should be slowed or accelerated.

## Takeaway
The document’s misspelling underscores the importance of precision in government communications, especially on high‑stakes issues like AI. While the pledge represents a voluntary step toward greater safety measures, its flawed language may affect its credibility and the public’s trust in future AI policy initiatives.
