## Launch Timing and Fee Structure
Morgan Stanley introduced its Bitcoin Trust in April 2026, followed by the Ethereum and Solana funds on July 28 of the same year. The three ETFs share one management team, a single fee of 0.14%, and one advisory group. The fee positions Morgan Stanley ahead of many other crypto funds, though advisers may weigh personal relationships more than cost.

## Fund Composition and Size
All three funds hold the underlying cryptocurrencies: Bitcoin (CRYPTO:BTC), Ethereum (CRYPTO:ETH) and Solana (CRYPTO:SOL). Bitcoin dominates in size, with U.S. spot Bitcoin funds holding about $111 billion. The combined lineup of Morgan Stanley’s ETFs exceeds $14 billion across 22 funds.

## Staking Versus Mining Rewards
The Ethereum and Solana ETFs stake their holdings and pass rewards directly to investors, with Morgan Stanley taking no cut. Bitcoin, however, operates on a mining system; its ETF does not provide staking rewards. This structural difference could influence adviser preferences, especially as Solana may see a greater impact per dollar invested.

## Potential Adviser Flow
Given Bitcoin’s established market presence and larger fund size, advisers may channel money into the Bitcoin ETF first. Yet, Solana’s higher reward potential per dollar could draw attention if investors prioritize yield. The single fee structure and unified management team suggest a streamlined decision process for advisers.

## What the Evidence Shows
- All three ETFs launched in mid‑2026 under one fee and management team. 
- Bitcoin leads in fund size, while Ethereum and Solana stake rewards. 
- The 0.14% fee is low relative to competitors, but advisers often prioritize relationships over cost.

Readers should monitor adviser allocations and staking reward flows to gauge which coin receives the first investment. The evidence underscores how fee structure, fund size, and reward mechanisms intertwine to shape capital movement within Morgan Stanley’s crypto offering.
