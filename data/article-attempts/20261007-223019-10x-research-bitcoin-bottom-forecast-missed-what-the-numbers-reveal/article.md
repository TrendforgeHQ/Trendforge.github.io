## Forecast vs. Reality
On June 24, Markus Thielen of 10x Research predicted a Bitcoin trough around $55,000 in late summer and early autumn 2026. Ten days later, on July 4, he tightened that range to $46,000–$47,000 and expected a 30 % rebound to $60,000–$65,000 by year‑end. The market did not follow those numbers. Bitcoin’s 2026 low was $57,717 on June 30, and the price later climbed 49 % to about $86,000—well above the bear target.

## Why the Gap Matters
With Bitcoin hovering near $86,000, reaching the $46,000–$47,000 level would have required a 24 %‑30 % drop by December 31. This scenario is far from the initial forecast and illustrates how quickly price dynamics can shift. Investors who sold near $59,000 around the first forecast missed a roughly 45 % increase in value.

## Methodological Additions
Thielen also expanded his analysis in July by applying Elliott Wave theory, a method that interprets price charts as repeating wave patterns. The addition of this framework did not alter the end‑of‑year target, but it did signal a more detailed approach to chart interpretation.

## Takeaway for Traders
The divergence between forecast and outcome highlights the risk of relying solely on analyst predictions. Monitoring actual price movements, especially when they diverge from projected ranges, remains essential. While analyst insights can guide expectations, they are not guarantees of market behavior.

## What the Evidence Shows
Bitcoin’s 2026 low of $57,717, followed by a 49 % rise to $86,000, eclipsed Markus Thielen’s $46,000–$47,000 bear target, underscoring the volatility inherent in cryptocurrency forecasting. Investors who liquidated near $59,000 experienced a missed 45 % gain, illustrating the practical impact of market swings on portfolio value.
