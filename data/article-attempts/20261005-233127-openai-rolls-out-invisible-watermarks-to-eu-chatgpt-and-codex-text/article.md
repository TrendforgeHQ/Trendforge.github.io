## What OpenAI Is Adding
OpenAI is starting to embed an invisible watermark in the text generated by ChatGPT and Codex for customers in the European Union. The watermark is not a visual mark; it is a statistical pattern created by slightly altering the model’s word choices. When a detector scans the output, it can pick up this pattern and flag the text as AI‑generated.

The initiative is part of OpenAI’s compliance with the EU AI Act, which requires providers of generative AI systems to identify AI‑generated content. The company will let API customers worldwide opt in to watermarking for selected models beginning today, while the EU rollout is slated for the coming weeks.

## How the Watermark Works and Its Limits
OpenAI’s textGrain approach relies on a secret key to sort next‑word predictions, producing a predictable statistical signature. Tests show that, at a 1% false‑positive target, the watermark is detected in about 80% of 200‑token psychology responses and roughly 95% of 400‑token passages. However, the technology is not perfect. Normal editing can weaken detection; replacing 10% of words with synonyms drops detection from about 92% to 66%, and a 25% substitution reduces it to 17%.

Short passages, math answers, and translated text are especially hard to detect, and the presence of a watermark does not confirm human authorship. A missing watermark could mean the text was too short, heavily edited, translated, or generated by a different AI model.

## Quality Impact and Availability
OpenAI reports that watermarking does not meaningfully affect the quality of GPT‑6 Astra. Benchmark results remain broadly similar when textGrain is enabled. The company is not making the watermark a global default at launch. Instead, it will offer access to its watermark detector to approved researchers and expert organizations initially, with plans to open the technology to the broader community.

The detector can identify that an OpenAI system produced or processed part of a passage, but it does not reveal who produced the text or how much human editing occurred. The watermark also does not identify the specific model that generated the content.

## What This Means for Users and Developers
For users in the EU, any text produced by ChatGPT or Codex will now carry this invisible mark. Developers using OpenAI’s API can opt in to receive watermarking for select models. The rollout is limited to the EU and will expand gradually; it is not a worldwide feature yet.

Because the watermark is statistical and can be weakened by editing, it should not be relied upon as a sole method for distinguishing AI from human text. Content creators should remain cautious, especially when publishing short or heavily edited pieces.

OpenAI’s decision to delay a global default reflects concerns that users might switch to rivals that do not watermark. The company plans to make the watermarking technology open source in the future, potentially allowing others to build on it.

## Key Takeaways
- OpenAI has begun adding invisible watermarks to ChatGPT and Codex text in the EU, as part of EU AI Act compliance.
- The watermark works by subtly altering word choices to create a detectable statistical pattern, but it is not perfect and can be weakened by editing.
- Detection rates are high for longer passages but drop significantly for shorter or heavily modified text.
- Watermarking does not affect output quality and is not yet a global default.
- API customers can opt in to watermarking, and OpenAI is opening the detector to researchers.

What the evidence shows is that OpenAI is taking a cautious, phased approach to watermarking AI‑generated text, balancing regulatory compliance with the practical limitations of the technology. Users in the EU can expect to see this feature soon, while developers worldwide will have the option to adopt it when it becomes available. The system’s effectiveness will continue to evolve as OpenAI refines the detection algorithm and expands access to the watermark detector.
