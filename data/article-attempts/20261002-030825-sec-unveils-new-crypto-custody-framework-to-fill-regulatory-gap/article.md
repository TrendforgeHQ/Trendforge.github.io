## A New Regulatory Path for Crypto Custody
The SEC’s latest proposal explicitly states it will “provide a clear regulatory framework for the custody of crypto assets, giving investment advisers and funds a compliant pathway where none existed before.” This marks the first time the agency has offered a specific route for advisers to hold digital‑asset holdings, a gap that has left many firms uncertain about compliance.

## Timing and Context
The announcement comes a day before Commissioner Hester Peirce, who has led the agency’s Crypto Task Force since its start, will leave. With the custody issue addressed, the SEC has now ticked off every major topic on the agenda originally outlined by Chairman Paul Atkins. The proposal also includes a rule that requires an adviser to prove no qualified custodian is available before self‑custody is permitted, and it mandates that firms demonstrate specific expertise in managing crypto assets.

## Broader Implications
Alongside the custody rule, the SEC has proposed a Regulation Crypto Asset that would explain how companies can raise funds through digital assets without violating federal securities law. These steps signal a shift toward clearer, more modern oversight of crypto markets, aligning regulatory language with the realities of digital‑asset ownership.

## Uncertainties and Next Steps
While the proposal outlines the framework, it does not yet indicate how the self‑custody requirement will be operationalized or which custodians may qualify under the new standards. Firms must monitor the SEC’s guidance for details on compliance thresholds and potential exemptions.

## What the Evidence Shows
The SEC has introduced a definitive custody framework for advisers, tying it to a broader crypto agenda and setting a new standard for self‑custody and fundraising via digital assets. This development removes a long‑standing regulatory blind spot and positions the U.S. market for clearer oversight of crypto‑asset custody practices.
