## Copilot Gains Local File Access
At the recent Windows and Surface event, Microsoft announced a new Copilot feature that lets the AI system read local files on a user’s PC and take actions across the operating system. The update was demonstrated during a live demo where the Copilot assistant helped a presenter file taxes.

This capability is part of Microsoft’s broader Hybrid Intelligence vision, which blends local and cloud‑based AI models to accomplish tasks efficiently.

## Hybrid Intelligence and Privacy Focus
Microsoft emphasized that Copilot’s local file access is opt‑in and that folder permissions are restricted. The agents run under separate accounts from the user, and the company highlighted agent workspaces and user‑controlled settings to keep privacy and security front and center. Hybrid Intelligence also allows some prompts to run against models on the computer, which can reduce privacy‑sensitive data exposure to the cloud.

The presentation also showcased a new Windows search experience that lets users perform quick actions directly from the search bar, such as turning on dark mode, adjusting mic volume with an inline slider, or sending a text.

## Impact for IT Teams
Alongside the new local‑file feature, Microsoft introduced the Copilot Control System, a set of tools designed to let IT teams confidently adopt and accelerate the business value of Copilot and its agents. The system will help IT leaders manage deployments, enforce policies, and monitor usage across an organization.

Microsoft plans to add many more agents in the coming year. These agents are intended to give customers a competitive advantage and help future‑proof their organizations.

## Early Adoption and Use Cases
Early adopters such as Dow, Bank of Queensland Group, and Eaton report significant operational gains from Copilot. Dow expects millions of dollars in savings on shipping operations in the first year. Bank of Queensland users say they are saving two and a half to five hours per week, and Eaton claims an 83 % speed‑up in internal documentation processes. Accenture is rolling Copilot out to 100,000 employees.

These examples illustrate how Copilot can reduce manual effort across diverse business functions.

## Uncertainties and Next Steps
Microsoft said features powered by Hybrid Intelligence will arrive over the next couple of months. While the company has highlighted privacy safeguards, the full scope of local file permissions and the extent of OS actions that Copilot can perform remain to be seen.

IT teams and power users should watch how the Copilot Control System evolves and how Microsoft balances local and cloud AI to protect data while delivering new productivity features.

## What the Evidence Shows
Microsoft is extending Copilot’s reach into local files and Windows system actions as part of its Hybrid Intelligence strategy, while emphasizing privacy controls and offering new management tools for IT. Early business cases point to substantial productivity gains, but the precise limits of local access and the timeline for broader rollout are still unfolding.
